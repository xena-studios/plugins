# xMarket 1.0: a server shop from 1.8.8, its price list made from recipes and proven loop-free (2026-11-27)

xMarket (M9, #110) is the family's first new plugin of the rewrite: `xmarket/api`, `xmarket/common`, `xmarket/bukkit` and `xmarket/fabric`, on the core's chest menus (decisions/2026-11-27-menus-in-the-core.md). What was decided:

- **The Bukkit floor is 1.8.8, as ROADMAP.md proposed.** The question was items: their ids changed at 1.13 (the flattening), so a price list keyed by today's ids needs 1.8 to 1.12's names and data values. Mojang's own data fixer has that mapping: run on 26.3's server jar, it turns a 1.12 item stack into today's, so `xmarket/prices/legacy.txt` (485 lines, 4 KiB in the jar) was made from it for every 1.12.2 material and data value, not by hand, and only a server before 1.13 reads it. Menus work on 1.8.8 as they are (the core's), and potions there are read from and made with their data values. The boot tests prove it: on Spigot 1.8.8 a real client opens `/shop`, buys a diamond through the menus with xMoney (and Vault, as 1.8.8 needs), sells it with `/sell hand`, buys stone with `/shop buy` and prices it with `/worth`, and `/shop audit` finds every item of the server priced.
- **The price list is one table of today's ids, made from base prices and recipes.** `xmarket/prices/base.txt` prices the 430-odd items nothing is made from, on one scale of how much time and risk an item takes in survival (1 a block dug by the stack, 5 coal, 20 iron, 200 a diamond, thousands for boss loot). Every other item costs the cheapest way to make it from priced items through any chain of recipes (`Pricing`, a fixed point over the recipes like Bellman-Ford, a recipe that takes its own result made net), rounded up to a cent; its sell price is 30% of that, rounded down. Enchanted books cost a book and the enchanting table's levels. 1,812 entries in 16 categories, one file each, in the registry's order so families stay together. Recipes come from Mojang's data generator (`xmarket/prices/extract.ts`), with what players do by hand (concrete, stripped logs, weathering, buckets, brewing) in `processes.txt`.
- **No loop, proven.** An item that sells for more than its cheapest way to be had (bought, or made from what's bought) is the end of any buy-craft-sell profit, however many steps it takes; `Audit.loops` finds every such item. `PriceListTest` runs it over the whole list against the recipes of 1.12.2, 1.13.2, 1.16.5, 1.20.1, 1.21.11 and 26.3 (older names through `ItemAliases`), and `/shop audit` runs it on a server's own prices, so an owner's edit that opens a loop is named with the recipe that makes it. The audit reads the newest version's recipes shipped in the jar, not the server's: a data pack's or another plugin's recipes aren't in it (a later version could read the server's, per platform).
- **Exact money.** `Money` in the API is units of 1/10,000, xMoney's own scale, so xMoney is paid in its units, never a double; Vault takes the nearest double at the edge. An item or level currency pays whole numbers, rounded up when buying and down when selling, on the total of a trade.
- **Dupe safety is the core menus' and the trade order.** Menus hold no real items; buying takes the money, then makes the items, refunding what didn't fit; selling takes the items, then pays, and gives them back if the currency refuses; a player leaving while the currency answers is refunded. One trade at a time per player, on their own thread (Folia's entity scheduler). `/sellgui` is a deposit, emptied as it closes for any reason.
- **No repeating tasks.** A limit's period starts again by comparing dates as it's read (a day by `rules.timezone`, a week from Monday); a chat prompt's timeout is one delayed task. History and limits are written off the game's threads, one writer, in order.
- **`/shop`'s words are one argument.** The core's commands take arguments or subcommands, not both, and `/shop <category>` is the spec's, so `/shop buy | history | stats | audit | edit` are words of the same argument, each checking its own permission (declared all the same). Their usage is in the docs and tab completion lists them by permission.
- **Startup.** Everything a server that's just started doesn't need is made on first use: the trades, wallets, discounts, limits, menus, importers and Bukkit's item code. On Paper 26.3 (M1 Pro, other boots running beside it, so loose): 34.8 ms startup with the startup list, 1.447 MB metaspace in its own loaders (against 1.5 MB: 1.564 MB before the trim), 2.4 to 4.2 MB heap over the server without it from run to run (321 KB of its own objects; the rest moves with the base server's), idle 0. CI's budgets job is the measure.
- **Jars over 500 KiB, recorded:** 778 KiB on Bukkit, 682 to 691 KiB on Fabric. About 400 KiB is the shared core (as xMoney's, xTeleport's and xBans'), about 300 KiB xMarket's own, and about 60 KiB data: the price list (24 KiB compressed), the newest recipes (17 KiB), the 1.8 to 1.12 names (4 KiB) and the translations (21 KiB). The price list is stored as the YAML an owner edits, one line per plain item, which compresses well; a binary form would save a few KiB at the cost of a second format.
- **Fabric.** Menus and commands with vanilla's own; money through xMoney, else the Common Economy API (compiled against, never carried). **Gaps:** requirements need a placeholder plugin, and there's none xMarket reads on Fabric, so an entry with requirements can't be used there; no EconomyShopGUI or ShopGUI+ on Fabric, so no import; no real client in its boot tests yet (boot-fabric.sh has no client step).
- **Not in 1.0:** Paper's dialogs for Bedrock players (the amount is typed in chat on every platform); prices pushed through the change feed to a network's other servers (a shared MySQL shares history and limits already); bStats, which has no service id for xMarket yet (none is sent); xStats' and xVote's integrations, which come with those plugins' rewrites. The brand catalog entry (glyph, headline) is the brand repo's, made before the release; until then the docs give xMarket its name (versions.json).
